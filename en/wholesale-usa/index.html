US shipments

What we confirm for every US order

We confirm US shipping order by order, so we do not publish fixed US delivery times, shipping prices or duty-free promises. For each order we agree the following with you:

  • Duties and tariffs. The expected rates for your products, based on their customs classification and origin.
  • Documents. Commercial invoice with product description and HS code, packing list, and a certificate of origin where it is needed.
  • Labelling and compliance. What the garment labels must show for sale in the US, such as fiber content, country of origin and manufacturer or registered identification.
  • Transit time and shipping cost. Carrier, service level, insurance and tracking.
  • Who pays what: DDP or DAP. With DDP (delivered duty paid) the duties and taxes are settled as part of the delivery. With DAP (delivered at place) you or your broker pay them on arrival. We agree which applies before we ship.
  • Importer of record. The party that files the customs entry and is responsible for the duties.
Good to know

US import basics for Moroccan kaftans

A short overview of what US buyers should know, as of November 25, 2026. It is general information and not legal or customs advice.

  • No more $800 exemption. US Customs and Border Protection suspended the $800 de minimis exemption for all modes of importation on June 24, 2026. Low-value shipments now need a customs entry and are subject to applicable duties, taxes and fees. Federal Register notice.
  • Additional duty on goods from Morocco. Since July 24, 2026 an additional Section 301 duty applies to many goods from Morocco, on top of the regular rate. Published sources put it at 12.5%; we treat the exact rate as something to confirm per shipment. Federal Register notice.
  • Free trade agreement. The US–Morocco Free Trade Agreement can allow duty-free treatment for textiles and apparel, but only if the rules of origin are met, which generally trace the yarn forward. We have not verified this for our products, so we make no promise of duty-free entry. Trade.gov summary.
  • Textile labels. The FTC’s textile rules generally require a label with fiber content, country of origin and the manufacturer’s name or registered identification number. FTC guidance.

Rules and rates change. Check current requirements with your customs broker or US Customs and Border Protection before ordering. We confirm duties, documents and labelling per order.
