How to choose a Moroccan bridal takchita

Style, colour, size and timing: how to choose a bridal takchita that suits you, with practical tips on fittings, planning and your whole celebration.

Home / Journal / How to choose a Moroccan bridal takchita

Published 25 November 2026 · 5 min read

One wedding, several looks

A Moroccan wedding is rarely a single moment. There is often a henna night, a ceremony or reception and a party evening, and brides regularly choose more than one outfit. So do not start with the garment, but with a question: which moments do I want to give their own look? For many brides the takchita is the highlight, because the two layers and the handwork feel so ceremonial.

Our bridal couture page shows what we make for brides: bridal kaftan, bridal takchita and bridal kimono.

Styles: from classic to modern

  • Classic and rich: plenty of embroidery, sfifa and aakad in gold or silver thread, often in a firm fabric such as brocade or velvet.
  • Light and soft: an airy outer layer in chiffon or mousseline with subtle embroidery, lovely if you want movement and a romantic silhouette.
  • Modern and clean: calm lines, fewer layers and one colour as the main theme. The handwork then lives in the details.

Choose a style that feels like you, not what you think is expected. You will wear this piece for hours, dance in it and be photographed in it.

Choosing colours

Traditionally, the henna night often calls for green and gold, and the main ceremony for white, ivory or champagne. Many brides also choose burgundy, emerald, royal blue or soft pink. There is no fixed rule. A few questions help:

  • Does the colour flatter your skin and hair? Ask for photos in daylight.
  • Does it suit your jewellery and belt: gold or silver?
  • Do you want the same colour world at every moment, or a different colour for each?

With made-to-measure, almost any design can be made in another colour or fabric.

Size and fit

A takchita is worn long and in several layers, so the fit decides how you feel. Measure yourself with the size guide and pay attention to shoulders, waist and length. Will you wear heels? Tell us, because the hem has to be matched to them.

  • Schedule the last fitting close to your wedding date, since measurements can change in the weeks before.
  • Test your range of movement: can you sit, raise your arms and dance?
  • For an order from a distance we work by video call and a measuring guide.

Timing: start early

A made-to-measure bridal piece takes time. We discuss the lead time per order via WhatsApp, plus time for fittings and shipping. Plan your first conversation at least 3 months before your wedding date. The earlier you start, the calmer it is, and spare time leaves room for a second fitting and small adjustments.

Is your deadline tighter? Do ask: sometimes it can be done faster, and a piece already in the collection has a shorter lead time. See also our shipping page.

Ordering from a distance

Do you live far from Morocco, in the Netherlands, Belgium, France or the Gulf states? Then we work through WhatsApp and video, with a measuring guide for your measurements. The fitting can also be done remotely. We ship internationally by express courier with tracking; costs and lead time are stated in your quote beforehand. Include shipping and any adjustments in your planning.

How we work on it together

  1. Introduction by WhatsApp or video: occasion, style, colours and budget, with inspiration photos if you have them.
  2. Design and quote: fabric, embroidery and details, with a clear quote.
  3. Production after a deposit of half, with photos of the progress.
  4. Fitting, finishing and safe shipping to your address.

Prefer not to have everything made? Some pieces can also be rented, for example for a second or third look.

Belt, jewellery and the rest of the look

The belt completes the takchita and shapes much of the silhouette. Choose gold or silver to match your jewellery and do not let the embroidery compete with statement necklaces. Think about shoes you can stand and dance in for hours, and about your hair: an updo works differently with the neckline than loose hair.

And the morning itself?

For getting ready on the morning of the wedding, many brides choose a bridal kimono instead of a takchita. You wear it during make-up and photos, and keep it afterwards. Read more in Bridal Kimonos: a new ritual.

Checklist for your first conversation

  • The date and place of the wedding, and which moments you want to dress for.
  • Colour ideas and photos of what you like.
  • Your measurements, or your height with and without heels.
  • Your budget and your ideal delivery date, well before the wedding.
